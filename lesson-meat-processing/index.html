SECTION A

Warm-up questions

Answer aloud in one or two sentences. Read an example after you try. Use personal examples only when they are true for you.

  1. Have you ever worked in a meat-processing factory?

    Example answer — use only if true: “Yes, I have worked in a meat-processing factory. I helped prepare and pack meat products.”

    Personalise your answer: If you have not done this work, say: “No, I have not worked in a meat-processing factory, but I am interested in learning about the work.” If you have, change the duties to match your real experience.

  2. What were your main responsibilities?

    Example answer — use only if true: “My main responsibilities were trimming meat, weighing portions, packing products and checking labels.”

    Personalise your answer: Name only duties you really performed. For example, remove any task you did not do.

  3. What types of meat or products did you handle?

    Example answer — use only if true: “I worked mainly with beef and pork. I trimmed the meat, weighed portions and packed the products.”

    Personalise your answer: Name only products you actually handled. If you handled chicken or other products instead, use their correct names.

  4. Did you work alone or as part of a team?

    Example answer — use only if true: “I worked as part of a team on a production line. We shared information and helped each other complete our tasks.”

    Personalise your answer: Say whether you usually worked alone, in a team, or both, and use a real example.

  5. What safety procedures did you follow?

    Example answer — use only if true: “I wore the required protective equipment, kept my work area tidy and followed the safety instructions. I asked my supervisor when I was unsure.”

    Personalise your answer: Mention only safety steps you actually followed at work.

SECTION C

Work-experience speaking practice

Try answering before you reveal the example. Past-work examples are labelled clearly; use them only if they are true for you.

1. Can you tell me about your previous work experience?

Example answer — use only if true: “I worked in a food-processing factory. I helped prepare and pack products, and I followed the workplace instructions.”

Personalise your answer: Name your real workplace or industry and describe the work you actually did.

2. How long did you work in meat processing?

Example answer — use only if true: “I worked in meat processing for two years. During that time, I learned to prepare and pack products carefully.”

Personalise your answer: Give your actual length of time and a skill you really learned. If you have not worked in meat processing, say so honestly and describe related experience instead.

3. What were your main duties?

Example answer — use only if true: “My main duties were trimming meat, weighing portions, packing products and checking labels.”

Personalise your answer: Keep only the duties you actually performed.

4. What equipment have you used?

Example answer — use only if true: “I have used a digital scale to weigh portions and a packing machine to seal packs. I followed the training and safety instructions.”

Personalise your answer: Name only equipment you have actually used. If you have not used factory equipment, say: “I have not used factory equipment yet, but I am willing to learn with proper training.”

5. How did you maintain hygiene?

Example answer — use only if true: “I washed my hands, wore the required protective equipment and kept my work area clean. I reported hygiene problems to my supervisor.”

Personalise your answer: Mention only the hygiene steps you really followed at work.

6. What challenges did you encounter?

Example answer — use only if true: “One challenge was working quickly while checking each product. I stayed focused and asked my supervisor when I needed help.”

Personalise your answer: Choose a challenge you really experienced and explain what you did.

7. How did you work with your colleagues?

Example answer — use only if true: “I worked with my colleagues by sharing information about our tasks and helping when the team was busy. I listened to instructions and asked questions if I was unsure.”

Personalise your answer: Add a real example of how you communicated or helped your team.

8. Why are you interested in working in Australia?

I plan to move to Australia with my husband. I hope to look for suitable meat-processing work where I can learn and contribute. I will check my visa conditions and work rights, and I understand that a job is not guaranteed.

Personalise your answer: Change your future plans if they are different from this example.

SECTION D

Food hygiene and workplace safety

Use these short explanations to practise giving a reason. Always follow the site's training and procedures.

Why is hygiene important in meat processing?

Hygiene is important because harmful germs can make people ill. Clean hands, tools and work areas help protect the food and the people who eat it.

How can contamination be prevented?

Workers can follow handwashing, cleaning and separation rules. They should use the correct tools for each task and report any possible contamination straight away.

Why must meat be stored at the required temperature?

Correct storage helps control food-safety risks and maintain product quality. I follow the workplace temperature requirements and instructions; I do not guess the temperature.

Why is protective equipment required?

The required PPE helps protect workers from hazards and helps meet workplace hygiene rules. I wear the equipment required for my task and use it correctly.

How should equipment be cleaned and sanitised?

I follow the site's cleaning instructions and use approved products in the correct way. I clean and sanitise equipment only when trained and authorised to do so.

Why must workers follow workplace procedures?

Procedures help protect workers and food, and help the team work consistently. If I do not understand a procedure, I ask my supervisor before continuing.

SECTION E

Workplace safety scenarios

Practise safe, calm responses. Your workplace's emergency and reporting procedures always come first.

1. A worker cuts their hand while trimming meat.

Speaking question: What would you do?

Useful vocabulary: cut, first aid, supervisor, report, procedure

I would stop work safely and tell the supervisor straight away. I would follow the workplace injury procedure and get help from a trained first aider. I would not ignore the cut or continue if it was unsafe.

Follow-up: Who should you tell about an injury at work?

2. You notice possible contamination on a product or work surface.

Speaking question: What would you do?

Useful vocabulary: contamination, separate, report, supervisor, procedure

I would stop and keep the affected product or area separate if the procedure says to do that. I would tell my supervisor immediately and follow the site's instructions. I would not decide on my own to use or discard the product.

Follow-up: Why should possible contamination be reported quickly?

3. A machine makes an unusual noise.

Speaking question: What would you do?

Useful vocabulary: unusual noise, equipment malfunction, stop, report, authorised

I would keep clear of danger and use the stop control only if I am trained and it is safe to do so. I would warn nearby workers and report the equipment malfunction. I would not open the machine or try an unauthorised repair.

Follow-up: What should you do if you have not been trained to use the stop control?

4. Liquid spills onto the factory floor.

Speaking question: What would you do?

Useful vocabulary: spill, wet floor, hazard, warn, clean-up procedure

I would warn people nearby and report the spill. I would follow the spill procedure and use a warning sign or clean-up method only as instructed and if I am trained. I would not leave the hazard unreported.

Follow-up: How could you warn a colleague about a wet floor?

5. You are asked to operate unfamiliar equipment.

Speaking question: What would you do?

Useful vocabulary: unfamiliar, training, instructions, supervisor, authorised

I would explain that I have not used the equipment before. I would ask my supervisor for the correct training and instructions, and wait until I am authorised. I would not guess how to operate it.

Follow-up: What could you say to your supervisor if you need training?

6. A colleague does not follow a hygiene procedure.

Speaking question: What would you do?

Useful vocabulary: hygiene, remind, respectfully, procedure, report

If it is appropriate, I would politely remind my colleague about the procedure. If the risk continues, I would report it to my supervisor and follow the workplace process. I would not ignore a food-safety risk.

Follow-up: How can you remind a colleague politely?

SECTION F

Employment interview role-play

Practise with a partner. Each example is a language model, not a claim about your background.

1. Can you tell me about your previous experience?

Example answer — use only if true: “I have worked in a food-processing workplace. I helped prepare and pack products, and I learned to follow hygiene instructions.”

Personalise your answer: Describe your actual workplace and tasks. If you do not have this experience, explain your real experience in another kind of work.

Follow-up questions: What did you learn there? Which task did you do most often?

Vocabulary: experience, task, learn

2. What duties did you do in your last job?

Example answer — use only if true: “My duties included trimming meat, weighing portions, packing products and checking labels. I followed the instructions for each task.”

Personalise your answer: Keep only duties you actually did.

Follow-up questions: Which duty did you enjoy most? Which duty required the most care?

Vocabulary: duties, instructions, carefully

3. How do you work as part of a team?

Example answer — use only if true: “I worked with a team on a production line. I shared information with my colleagues and asked for help when I needed it.”

Personalise your answer: Use a real example of teamwork from your own experience.

Follow-up questions: How do you help a teammate? What do you do if you do not understand a request?

Vocabulary: teammate, communicate, share

4. What do you do to maintain good hygiene?

Example answer — use only if true: “I wash my hands, wear the required protective equipment and keep my work area clean. I report hygiene concerns to my supervisor.”

Personalise your answer: Mention only hygiene steps you really follow or have followed at work.

Follow-up questions: Why is hygiene important? What would you do if you noticed contamination?

Vocabulary: hygiene, contamination, sanitise

5. How do you keep yourself and others safe at work?

I would wear the required PPE and follow the safety procedures for each task. If I noticed a hazard, I would keep clear and report it to my supervisor.

Follow-up questions: What PPE have you used? What would you do if you saw a spill?

Vocabulary: PPE, hazard, safety procedure

6. Are you comfortable working different shifts?

Example answer — use only if true: “I am available for morning and afternoon shifts. I would like to understand the roster and shift times for this role.”

Personalise your answer: State your real availability; do not agree to shifts you cannot work.

Follow-up questions: What hours are you available? How do you prepare for an early shift?

Vocabulary: shift, roster, availability

7. What do you do if you do not understand an instruction?

I ask the supervisor to explain or show me the task again. I repeat the key instruction to check that I understand before I continue.

Follow-up questions: What words could you use to ask for clarification? Why is checking important?

Vocabulary: clarify, instruction, confirm

8. How do you approach learning new equipment?

I am willing to learn. I would read or listen to the instructions, complete the required training and practise only when authorised and supervised as required.

Follow-up questions: Have you learned a new task before? What support helps you learn?

Vocabulary: training, equipment, authorised

9. What would you do if a machine stopped working?

I would follow the site procedure, keep clear of danger and report the problem to my supervisor. I would not try to repair the machine unless I was trained and authorised.

Follow-up questions: Who would you notify? Why should workers avoid unauthorised repairs?

Vocabulary: malfunction, report, repair

10. Why are you interested in this job?

I am interested in this job because I would like to work as part of a team and learn the requirements of this workplace. I would follow the training and safety instructions.

Personalise your answer: Add your genuine reason for applying. Mention past experience only if it is true.

Follow-up questions: What can you bring to the team? What would you like to learn?

Vocabulary: motivated, experience, learn

SECTION G

Partner-visa context

Partner-visa applications concern the genuine relationship and relevant visa circumstances. Occupational knowledge is not automatically tested, and an interview is not guaranteed in every case. The questions below are general English practice only; they are not official immigration interview questions.

This lesson is not legal advice. A partner visa does not by itself guarantee a particular job. Check your own visa conditions and work rights through official Australian government services before accepting work.

How do you feel about moving to Australia with your husband?

My husband and I plan to move to Australia together. We will support each other as we settle into our new home.

Personalise your answer: Describe your real plans and feelings about the move.

What are your plans after you arrive?

After I arrive, I plan to settle in with my husband, learn about local services and check my visa conditions before making work plans.

Personalise your answer: Change these plans if your actual plans are different.

What are your employment intentions?

I hope to look for suitable meat-processing work if my visa conditions allow me to work. I understand that employment depends on available jobs and an employer's decision; a job is not guaranteed.

Personalise your answer: Describe your genuine employment plans and check your own visa work conditions.

SECTION H

Final speaking test

Answer all questions aloud without looking at the sample answers first. Aim for two to four sentences. Past-work examples are labelled; use them only if true and personalise them with accurate details.

1. Tell me about your work experience.

Example answer — use only if true: “I worked in a food-processing workplace. I helped prepare and pack products, and I learned to follow hygiene instructions.”

Personalise your answer: Give your real work history, length of experience and duties.

2. What were your main job duties?

Example answer — use only if true: “My main duties were trimming meat, weighing portions, packing products and checking labels. I followed the work instructions and checked my work.”

Personalise your answer: Name only duties you actually performed.

3. Explain three meat-processing words you have learned.

“Trim” means to remove unwanted parts of meat. “Label” means a product tag or the action of adding it. “Sanitise” means to reduce germs on a surface using an approved method.

4. Why are hygiene and safe storage important?

Good hygiene and correct storage help control food-safety risks and protect product quality. Workers should follow workplace rules and store meat at the required temperatures.

5. What would you do if you saw a hazard at work?

I would keep myself and others safe, warn people if needed and report the hazard to my supervisor. I would follow the site's procedure.

6. What would you say if you did not understand your supervisor?

I would say, “Could you please explain that again?” Then I would repeat the instruction to check that I understood correctly.

7. How would you respond to unfamiliar equipment?

I would not operate it until I had the correct training and permission. I would ask my supervisor for instructions.

8. What are your future employment plans in Australia?

I hope to look for suitable meat-processing work if my visa conditions allow me to work. I will check the requirements before applying, and I understand that a job is not guaranteed.

Personalise your answer: Change your employment plans if your genuine plans are different.
