WEEK 3 · A2–B1

Quality Control and Product Standards

Learn to describe a concern clearly and report it to the right person using workplace instructions.

← Previous: Week 2Course overviewNext: Week 4 →

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Understand why products may be checked against standards.
  • Describe damaged packaging or a possible product defect.
  • Report a concern and ask what to do next.

This lesson teaches communication, not inspection or food-safety procedures. Follow your workplace training and report concerns to the responsible supervisor.

USEFUL WORKPLACE EXPRESSIONS

Describe and report a concern

  • “This product doesn’t meet the required standards.”
  • “The packaging is damaged.”
  • “I noticed a problem with this batch.”
  • “Should I report this to the supervisor?”
  • “This product needs further inspection.”

READING

A concern during a product check

Quality control means checking products against the standards used at a workplace. The checks and responsibilities vary between sites and jobs. A worker may be asked to look at packaging, a label or another feature described in their training. During a task, a worker notices that one package is damaged. The worker does not make assumptions about what caused the damage or decide alone what should happen to the product.

Instead, the worker reports the concern to the supervisor and gives clear information. This may include where the item was found and, if available and requested, its batch number. The supervisor explains the next step according to the site’s procedure. A product may need further inspection or may be handled as a rejected product, depending on the trained process. Quality checks help teams notice problems and keep products consistent. Food safety also matters because contamination can make food unsafe. Workers should follow their role-specific training and report anything that concerns them. Clear, calm communication helps the team respond and gives the responsible person a chance to decide what action is needed. This lesson does not replace workplace instructions.

  3. Who explains the next step?
Show suggested answers
  1. Checking products against workplace standards.
  2. Report the concern clearly to the supervisor and give relevant details.

SPEAKING PRACTICE

Give a clear report

  1. Describe a package with a damaged corner. What do you notice?
  2. Report a problem with one batch to a supervisor.
  3. Explain why product checks and consistent standards are important.
  4. Practise asking what to do next without guessing about a procedure.

Seasonal conversation — Christmas food: What food do you prepare for Christmas? Why is food safety important when preparing food for a large family gathering?

WORKPLACE ROLE-PLAY

Reporting damaged packaging

Situation: During a task, you notice a package is damaged. Tell the supervisor what you observed and ask what to do.

You: “I noticed a problem with this batch. The packaging on this product is damaged.”

Supervisor: “Thanks for telling me. Please follow the site procedure while I check the details.”

You: “Should I report the batch number as well?”

Supervisor: “Yes, please give me the information shown on the label.”

Practise with another issue, such as a missing label. Report only what you can see; let the supervisor decide the next action under workplace procedures.

HOMEWORK & SHADOWING

Practise a short report

Read the role-play aloud. Then cover the text and give a 20-second report about a pretend packaging problem: say what you observed, where it is, and ask what to do next. Record yourself and repeat, speaking clearly and calmly.
