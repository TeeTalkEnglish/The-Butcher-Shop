A Typical Working Day in Australia

Practise discussing shifts, rosters, breaks, overtime and friendly everyday conversations at work.

← Previous: Week 3Course overview

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Talk about Australian workplace routines and shift arrangements.
  • Ask clear questions about a roster, breaks and overtime.
  • Use everyday workplace language with coworkers and a supervisor.

Work hours, breaks, employment conditions and rosters depend on the job and workplace. Check your own roster, employment information and workplace policies.

USEFUL WORKPLACE EXPRESSIONS

Ask about your hours

  • “What time does my shift start?”
  • “Where can I check my roster?”
  • “Am I working this Saturday?”
  • “Is overtime available?”
  • “How long is our lunch break?”
  • “Who should I report to?”
  • “I’ve finished my shift.”

READING

Getting ready for a shift

Before a new week, a worker checks the roster to see the days and times they are scheduled to work. If the roster is not clear, they ask their supervisor where to find the latest information. On a workday, the worker follows the workplace process for recording the start of the shift. The team may have a handover so that important work information is shared with the next shift. Workers can ask who they should report to and what their first task is.

Break times and arrangements depend on the workplace and the worker’s employment conditions. If a worker has a question, they can ask their supervisor or check the information provided by their employer. Overtime may sometimes be available, but workers should check what applies to their role rather than assume. A payslip shows pay information; ask the employer if something is unclear. In Australia, people may work full-time, part-time or in casual employment, and the details can differ. At the end of a shift, the worker completes the site’s finish-time process and tells the appropriate person that their shift is finished. Clear questions help everyone understand the arrangements.

Check your understanding

  1. What does a roster show?
  2. Who can you ask if the break arrangements are unclear?
  3. Why should a worker check their own employment information?
Show suggested answers
  1. It shows when employees are scheduled to work.
  2. Ask the supervisor or check the information provided by the employer.
  3. Arrangements differ between workplaces and employment conditions.

SPEAKING PRACTICE

Talk about a working week

  1. Describe a sample weekly schedule using days and times.
  2. Ask a supervisor when your shift starts and where to check your roster.
  3. Role-play asking a coworker about the lunch break.
  4. Describe a typical working day from arriving to finishing your shift.

New Year conversation: What are your goals for next year? What are you looking forward to in Australia? What skills would you like to improve?

WORKPLACE ROLE-PLAY

Checking the roster

Situation: You are not sure whether you are working on Saturday. Ask your supervisor to help you check the roster.

You: “Could I check my roster with you, please? Am I working this Saturday?”

Supervisor: “Let’s check the current roster together.”

You: “Thanks. What time does my shift start, and who should I report to?”

Supervisor: “Your start time and team contact are listed here. Please ask me if anything is unclear.”

Repeat the role-play using questions about overtime or a break. Check the actual details with your workplace rather than assuming an arrangement.

HOMEWORK & SHADOWING

Practise a roster conversation

Write a pretend workweek with three shifts. Read the days and times aloud, then practise asking a supervisor to confirm one shift, a break and who to report to. Shadow each question three times, keeping your voice clear and polite.
