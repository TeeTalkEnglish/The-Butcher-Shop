WEEK 2 · A2–B1

Following Workplace Instructions

Practise listening, checking what you heard and communicating clearly with supervisors and coworkers.

← Previous: Week 1Course overviewNext: Week 3 →

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Understand and check instructions from a supervisor.
  • Ask a coworker for help or clarification politely.
  • Talk through simple steps and confirm when a task is finished.

Use the current workplace procedure and training. If an instruction is unclear, stop and ask the responsible person rather than guessing.

USEFUL WORKPLACE EXPRESSIONS

Listen, ask and confirm

  • “Could you show me how to do this?”
  • “Could you repeat that, please?”
  • “What should I do next?”
  • “Where should I put these products?”
  • “I’ve finished this task.”
  • “I’m not sure I understand.”
  • “Could you explain that again?”

READING

Checking the task

At the beginning of a shift, a supervisor may tell the team about the day’s tasks and production schedule. The worker checks which task to do first and asks where products or materials should go. Instructions can be difficult to hear in a busy workplace, so it is sensible to ask the supervisor to repeat or explain them. It is better to check than to guess.

Before starting a task, the worker follows the training and the procedure for that job. If equipment or a step is unfamiliar, the worker asks for help from the supervisor or an appropriate trained coworker. A checklist may help the team remember what they need to review. After completing a task, the worker tells the supervisor and asks what to do next. Clear communication helps coworkers coordinate their work. A polite question is a normal part of learning a new job. If you do not understand an instruction, say so calmly. Repeat the main point back in your own words and wait for confirmation. Each workplace has its own procedures, so do not rely on an example from another site instead of your own training.

Check your understanding

  1. Who may explain the day’s tasks?
  2. What should a worker do if an instruction is difficult to hear?
  3. Why should a worker avoid guessing about an unfamiliar step?
Show suggested answers
  1. The supervisor may explain the tasks.
  2. Ask the supervisor to repeat or explain it.
  3. The worker should follow the correct training and procedure, and ask for help if unsure.

SPEAKING PRACTICE

Ask for help confidently

  1. Practise asking someone to repeat an instruction politely.
  2. Explain how you would check what to do next.
  3. Give a simple three-step instruction for an everyday task, such as making a cup of tea.
  4. Say how you would tell your supervisor that your task is finished.

Seasonal conversation — Christmas: How do you usually spend Christmas? Would you like to celebrate Christmas with Australian coworkers? What are your plans for Christmas?

WORKPLACE ROLE-PLAY

A new task on shift

Situation: Your supervisor gives you a task, but you did not hear where the products should go. Ask for clarification and repeat the answer to check.

Supervisor: “Please check the task list and move the completed items to the area shown in your instructions.”

You: “Could you show me where I should put these products, please?”

Supervisor: “I’ll show you the location and explain the site instructions.”

You: “Thank you. So I follow the instructions, then tell you when I’ve finished. Is that right?”

Take turns being the supervisor and new employee. Practise asking someone to repeat, explain or demonstrate an unfamiliar task. Do not invent steps.

HOMEWORK & SHADOWING

Practise useful questions

Choose four expressions from this lesson. Say each one aloud three times, slowly and then at a natural speed. Record a short supervisor-and-worker conversation. Listen back and practise once more, focusing on a clear, polite question.
